AI-generated summary

Can RL recover a rule we already understand?

Before asking reinforcement learning (RL) to discover a better network controller, this paper asks whether it can reliably recover a strong, simple heuristic. In adaptive bitrate streaming (ABR), a familiar rule is to select higher video quality as the playback buffer grows. A compact policy can express this behavior with just a few parameters, making it possible to inspect how training succeeds or fails.

The heuristic supplies a target behavior and a performance bar. Training receives only rewards, with no examples of the heuristic’s decisions. Passing the test requires suitable behavior, comparable performance, and reliability across training runs.

Three views make training inspectable

  1. Return landscape: Does the reward make the desired behavior attractive?
  2. Optimization path: Does training reach a useful part of that landscape?
  3. Policy behavior: Does the learned controller satisfy the intended rule?

The demo brings these views together: the heatmap shows expected return, the orange path traces training, and the policy curves show bitrate choices as the buffer fills. A rising reward alone cannot answer all three questions.

Similar symptoms, different causes

In an optimization failure, a useful solution exists but training misses it. An unfavorable starting point can lead toward a flat region; an excessive learning rate can overshoot a useful ridge. Even this small policy space has ridges and plateaus.

In an objective failure, the reward or training traces favor undesirable behavior. Penalizing stalls too weakly can favor aggressive bitrate choices. Changing the optimizer cannot repair that incentive: the reward or training distribution must change.

A diagnostic starting point

The paper studies controlled ABR experiments and additional congestion-control examples. The browser demo uses two bitrates, short episodes, and seven constant-bandwidth traces, allowing expected return to be calculated exactly. It trains and evaluates on those same traces.

Rediscovering a heuristic is a minimum capability check. One successful run does not establish reliability, generalization to real networks, or the performance of a larger neural policy.
