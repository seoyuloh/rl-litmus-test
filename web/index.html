How reward works

Reward per chunk = bitrate / 4.3 − stall penalty × stall seconds / 4

Higher bitrate earns more reward; waiting for video subtracts reward. Bitrate is in Mbps, and each chunk is 4 seconds.

At penalty 4.3, each second of stalling subtracts 1.075 reward.

Return on the heatmap is the expected sum of rewards over 8 chunks, averaged across 7 constant-bandwidth network traces: 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5, 5.5, and 6 Mbps.

How to read this figure

Left: each point is a policy with weight w and bias b. Its color shows the expected return in the controlled ABR simulation. Training moves the policy across this map, which expands automatically when needed. Clicking it starts a new experiment at that point.

Right: the two curves show that policy’s probability of choosing each bitrate. Buffer occupancy is how full the video buffer is; 100% means 60 seconds ready to play. The summary describes the preferred choice, breaking ties toward the lower bitrate.

Training uses eight video chunks and seven constant-bandwidth network traces. A run now lasts 10,000 updates. Continue adds another 10,000 without resetting the policy; Reset or a setting change starts a fresh run. The freezing penalty changes the scoring rule, so compare returns within one run. A rising return alone does not establish correct behavior or reliability.
